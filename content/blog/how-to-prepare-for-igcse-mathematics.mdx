IGCSE Mathematics rewards steady, organised preparation more than last-minute cramming. The syllabus is wide but predictable: the same ideas appear year after year, dressed in slightly different numbers. Here is how to prepare so the exam feels familiar.

## Know what the papers test

IGCSE Mathematics is usually examined in two papers: one without a calculator and one with. Both test the same syllabus — number, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, mensuration, vectors, probability and statistics. Papers mix short routine questions with longer multi-step problems. The method marks matter: even a wrong final answer earns credit for correct working, so **never skip steps.**

## Phase 1: Rebuild chapter by chapter (weeks 1–4)

Go through the syllabus one chapter at a time. For each chapter:

1. Read the lesson and note the key methods in your own words.
2. Work the examples with the solutions covered.
3. Do practice questions until you can solve them without looking.

Start with [Algebra](/subjects/mathematics/cambridge/igcse/algebra) — it underpins half the paper. Make sure [linear equations](/subjects/mathematics/cambridge/igcse/algebra/linear-equations) and [quadratic equations](/subjects/mathematics/cambridge/igcse/algebra/quadratic-equations) are genuinely solid before moving on; weak algebra leaks marks into every other topic.

## Phase 2: Mixed and timed practice (weeks 5–7)

Stop revising by chapter. Do mixed question sets so you practise *choosing* the method, not just executing it. Then introduce the clock: past papers under timed conditions, no notes, no phone. Mark harshly against the mark scheme and log every lost mark in a mistake book with the reason in your own words.

## Phase 3: Target the gaps (final weeks)

Your mistake book is now your syllabus. Re-do every logged error. Re-sit one or two full papers to rebuild exam stamina. In the last days, revise formulas and methods only — no new topics.

## Exam-day habits that save marks

- **Show every step.** Method marks are free points for visible working.
- **Check by substitution.** Plug answers back into equations; re-read word problems against your answer.
- **Manage the clock.** If a question stalls for more than two minutes, move on and return later. Easy marks at the end of the paper beat heroic struggles in the middle.
- **Units and precision.** Read what the question asks for — "give your answer to 3 significant figures" is an instruction, not a suggestion.

## The mindset

Students who do well at IGCSE Mathematics are not the ones who never make mistakes — they are the ones who found their mistakes early and fixed them. Start with one chapter today, work it properly, and let the plan carry you from there.
