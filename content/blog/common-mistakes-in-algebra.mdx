Algebra mistakes are rarely about not understanding. Most of the time you know the method — the marks slip away in small, repeatable slips. Here are the ones teachers see most, and the habit that fixes each.

## 1. Sign errors when moving terms

The classic. Solving `x − 7 = 12`, a student writes `x = 12 − 7` and gets 5 instead of 19. The rule is mechanical: **whatever crosses the equals sign changes sign.** Say it out loud as you write each line. If you are shaky on the basics, revisit [variables](/subjects/mathematics/cambridge/igcse/algebra/variables) and how terms behave first.

## 2. Forgetting to multiply every term in brackets

Expanding `3(x + 4)` as `3x + 4` instead of `3x + 12`. The number outside the bracket must visit *every* term inside — draw a small arrow to each term if you have to. Practise with the [expressions](/subjects/mathematics/cambridge/igcse/algebra/expressions) lesson until expanding feels automatic.

## 3. Doing different operations to each side

Solving `2x + 6 = 14`, a student subtracts 6 from the left but forgets the right, or divides only one term. An equation is a balance: **every step applies to the whole of both sides.** Write each step on its own line so you can see the full equation at every stage. The [linear equations](/subjects/mathematics/cambridge/igcse/algebra/linear-equations) lesson walks through this line by line.

## 4. Treating letters like they are all the same

`3x + 2y` cannot become `5xy`. Only like terms combine — same letter, same power. When you are tempted to merge unlike terms, stop and ask: "same letter?" If not, leave it alone.

## 5. Rushing the check

The single highest-value habit in algebra: **substitute your answer back into the original equation.** If you found `x = 5` for `2x + 6 = 16`, check `2(5) + 6 = 16`. Ten seconds, and it catches four of the five mistakes above. Students who check consistently score a full grade higher on equation questions — not because they are smarter, but because they catch their own slips.

## How to train these out

Do not just read this list. Take five old problems you got wrong, redo them slowly, and for each one write down which of the five mistakes it was. Patterns appear fast — most students find 80% of their errors come from just one or two habits. Fix those two, and your algebra transforms.
