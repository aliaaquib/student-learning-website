## Key ideas

### Conjugating -ar verbs

Remove -ar and add the endings -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an. So hablar gives hablo (I speak), hablas (you speak), habla (he/she speaks). Subject pronouns are usually dropped — hablo already means 'I speak'.

### Ser versus estar

Use ser for what something is: identity, origin, profession, time (Soy médico — I am a doctor). Use estar for how or where something is: location, mood, temporary states (Estoy en casa — I am at home; Estoy contenta — I am happy). Remember: ser is essence, estar is state.

**Key term — estar:** To be (location, temporary states, feelings): estoy, estás, está, estamos, estáis, están — e.g. Estoy cansada (I am tired).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Ser and Estar">
  Choose ser or estar: '___ de Madrid' (I am from Madrid).

  Soy de Madrid (ser).

  **Answer:** Soy de Madrid (ser).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Using ser for feelings** Estoy cansado (I am tired) uses estar — with ser it would mean you are a tiring person by nature!
  - **Saying 'yo hablo' every time** Drop the pronoun — hablo is enough; overusing 'yo' sounds unnatural.
</ImportantNote>
