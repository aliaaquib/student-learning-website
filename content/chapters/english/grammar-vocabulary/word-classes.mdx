## Key ideas

### Vary your sentence types

Simple sentences hit hard. Compound sentences link equal ideas with and, but or so. Complex sentences use subordinate clauses to show how ideas connect — because, although and when reveal cause and contrast. Mixing all three keeps writing lively; using only one type sounds robotic.

### Punctuation controls meaning

Commas separate items and clauses so readers know where ideas begin and end. Compare 'Let's eat, Grandma!' with 'Let's eat Grandma!' — one comma decides whether Grandma joins dinner or becomes it. Punctuate deliberately, not by guesswork.

**Key term — clause:** A group of words containing a verb. A main clause makes sense alone; a subordinate clause needs the rest of the sentence.

<WorkedExample title="Fix the punctuation">
  Add the missing punctuation: its raining so we cant play outside said Tom.

  Find the speech: the words Tom actually says are its raining so we cant play outside.
  Add inverted commas around the speech and capitalise its first word.
  Fix its to it's — it means it is, so the apostrophe shows the missing letter.
  Fix cant to can't for the same reason.
  Add a comma before the closing inverted commas: "It's raining so we can't play outside," said Tom.

  **Answer:** It's raining so we can't play outside, said Tom — with inverted commas around the speech: "It's raining so we can't play outside," said Tom.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Confusing its and it's** It's always means it is or it has. If you cannot replace it with it is, write its with no apostrophe.
  - **Comma splices** Joining two main clauses with only a comma is an error: It rained, we stayed in. Use a full stop, and, or a semicolon instead.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Identify the subordinate clause: Although it rained, we played on." hint="Which part cannot stand alone?">
    Although it rained.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What are the two jobs of the apostrophe?" hint="Possession and...">
    Showing possession and showing missing letters.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Correct this comma splice: The bell rang, everyone left." hint="Try a full stop.">
    The bell rang. Everyone left. (Equally correct: The bell rang, so everyone left.).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which is correct: the dogs bone or the dog's bone?" hint="The bone belongs to the dog.">
    The dog's bone.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
