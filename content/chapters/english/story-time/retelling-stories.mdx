<Definition term="Retelling Stories">
  Tell a story back in your own words, keeping the events in order.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Listening is a skill

Good listeners sit still, look at the storyteller and keep the story in their heads. You can show you are listening by joining in with repeated phrases, such as roaring along with the giant. Listening well is the first step to understanding stories.

### Stories follow a pattern

Most stories introduce a character and a setting, then something goes wrong in the middle, and the ending sorts it out. Spotting this beginning-middle-end pattern helps you remember stories and tell your own.

**Key term — character:** A person, animal or creature in a story. You can describe how a character looks, feels and behaves.

<WorkedExample title="Retell: The Lost Duckling">
  Listen to this short story, then retell it in order. Story: A little duckling wandered away from the pond. It felt lost and lonely. A kind frog showed it the way home. The duckling's mother hugged it tight.

  Name the beginning: the duckling wanders away from the pond.
  Name the middle: the duckling feels lost, then the kind frog helps.
  Name the ending: the duckling comes home and its mother hugs it.
  Tell it back using the words first, then and finally.

  **Answer:** First the duckling wandered away from the pond. Then it felt lost and lonely until a kind frog showed it the way home. Finally its mother hugged it tight.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Telling events out of order** Use the words first, then and finally to keep the beginning, middle and end in the right order.
  - **Forgetting to name the characters** Say who the story is about at the start, for example: This story is about a little duckling.
</ImportantNote>
